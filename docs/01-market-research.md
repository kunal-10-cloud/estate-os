# Build the real estate bundle around one record

Real estate software is sold today as five separate stacks that rarely share data. The first is agent CRMs and lead generation, led by Follow Up Boss, BoldTrail, Lofty, Real Geeks, CINC and Ylopo. The second is transaction and back office: SkySlope, Lone Wolf, dotloop, Paperless Pipeline and ListedKit. The third is property management: AppFolio, Buildium, DoorLoop, Yardi Breeze, Rent Manager, Entrata, RentRedi and Guesty. The fourth is developer and new-homes sales and ERP: Sell.Do, Farvision, Lasso/BuildTopia and Zonda. The fifth is regional broker CRMs such as PropSpace, Goyzer, MRI Vault and Rex. In the sources we could verify, only Inside Real Estate's BoldTrail covers front office, back office, recruiting and AI natively, and its new mobile app averages **1.46 stars across 37 recent written reviews**. In other words, the most complete suite is also the one users complain about most. Complaints repeat across almost every vendor: mobile apps that can't do what the desktop does, contracts that are hard to exit, upsells, data trapped in separate silos, and AI that gets in the way of simple tasks. A modular bundle can win by matching the leader feature-for-feature in each module (the checklist in section 3 sets that bar). Its real edge would come from four things incumbents structurally lack: one shared record for each person, household, property and deal across every module; full parity between the mobile and desktop apps; deal objects built for the post-NAR-settlement rules; and transparent month-to-month pricing with data that stays with the user. Web search was unavailable during the research, so evidence comes from vendor pricing pages, App Store reviews, GOV.UK, NAR and Wikipedia. Several major vendors are covered only partly (Zillow Premier Agent, dotloop tiers, Lone Wolf modules, the Gulf portals, Lasso/BuildTopia). Every claim the notes could not verify sits in the "Known gaps" section and nowhere else.

## Five segments, three architectures, almost no shared data

The agent market splits into three architectures. **Pure CRM hubs** such as Follow Up Boss (FUB) and Wise Agent connect to "any lead source and website" and do not sell IDX sites or ads ([FUB pricing](https://www.followupboss.com/pricing)). **All-in-one front offices** combine an IDX website, paid lead generation, a CRM and an AI texting or voice ISA (inside sales agent). This group includes Lofty, Real Geeks, CINC, Ylopo and BoldTrail ([Lofty pricing](https://lofty.com/pricing); [Real Geeks](https://www.realgeeks.com/); [CINC pricing](https://www.cincpro.com/pricing); [Ylopo pricing](https://www.ylopo.com/pricing)). **Brokerage operating systems** reach into commissions, accounting and recruiting. BoldTrail pairs its front office with BackOffice (agent billing, onboarding, QuickBooks, commission management) and Recruit (recruiting dashboard, transaction heat map, net-income valuation tool) ([boldtrail.com](https://www.boldtrail.com/)). Rechat sells four modules (Studio, People, Deals and the Lucy AI assistant) that brokerages can adopt one at a time ([Rechat pricing](https://rechat.ai/pricing/)). Among CRMs, only Rechat Deals and Wise Agent include transaction management natively ([Wise Agent pricing](https://wiseagent.com/pricing/)).

The deal-to-close layer is itself three layers. The first is forms and e-sign: SkySlope Forms/DigiSign and Lone Wolf's Authentisign, which handles about **35 million signings a year** ([Lone Wolf](https://www.lwolf.com/)). The second is the compliance file: checklists, audit review and the broker dashboard. The third is back-office money: commission plans, caps, CDAs (commission disbursement authorizations) and accounting. SkySlope sells all three as a modular suite to **900,000+ professionals handling 3M+ transactions a year** ([SkySlope](https://skyslope.com/)). Paperless Pipeline sells the compliance and commission layers to SMB brokerages ([Paperless Pipeline](https://www.paperlesspipeline.com/pricing)). ListedKit aims AI at transaction coordinators (TCs): its "Ava" assistant reads contracts and builds timelines ([ListedKit](https://listedkit.com/pricing)). Zillow is the only company covering most of the agent's deal journey. It owns showings and offers (ShowingTime), transactions (dotloop, which says it touches **over 50% of US transactions** ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/dotloop/))) and the CRM (FUB) ([ShowingTime](https://www.showingtime.com/)). Qualia leads title and escrow, and its agentic "Clear 2.0" claims to automate **80% of title and escrow work** ([Qualia](https://www.qualia.com/)). Homebot owns post-close homeowner nurture, with **13M homeowners and 10,000+ customer organizations** ([Homebot](https://homebot.ai/)).

Property management software shares one core across small and mid-sized US vendors: listings, screening, leases, rent collection, a tenant portal, maintenance and accounting. Vendors hold back **trust accounting, owner portals, inspections, APIs and AI** for higher tiers. For example, Rentec includes trust accounting and owner portals only on its PM plan ([Rentec](https://www.rentecdirect.com/pricing)). Enterprise suites add leasing CRMs, affordable and student housing, budgeting, read/write APIs and agentic AI. Examples are AppFolio Max ([AppFolio](https://www.appfolio.com/pricing)) and Entrata ELI+, whose four agents handle leasing, payments, renewals and maintenance ([Entrata](https://www.entrata.com/platforms/eli)). Short-term rentals are a separate category built around a channel manager ([Guesty](https://www.guesty.com/pricing/)).

Developer software looks different everywhere else. In India it is a two-layer stack. A real-estate CRM (Sell.Do, with **1,500+ businesses** including Hiranandani, Shapoorji Pallonji and Emaar) runs pre-sales, site visits, channel partners (CPs), WhatsApp and the IRIS launch-day booking engine ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/)). A builder ERP (Farvision, **900+ firms**) runs demand letters, collections, RERA, GST and TDS reporting, possession and procurement ([Farvision](https://www.farvisionerp.com/)). IRIS shows what launch-day software looks like in practice. It collects EOIs (expressions of interest) with tokens, runs eKYC and Digilocker checks, shares a live unit grid with CPs, and runs a "command centre". Sell.Do cites a case of **"400+ units in 6 hours" with zero double bookings** ([Sell.Do IRIS](https://www.sell.do/iris)). In the UAE, broker CRMs revolve around syndication to Property Finder, Bayut and dubizzle ([PropSpace](https://www.propspace.com/); [Goyzer](https://www.goyzer.com/)). Australia and New Zealand have consolidated: MRI Vault claims **more than 1 in 4 ANZ agencies** ([MRI Vault](https://www.mrisoftware.com/au/products/vault/)), and Rex serves Harcourts, LJ Hooker and RE/MAX ([Rex](https://www.rexsoftware.com/)). US builders split their spend between builder CRM/ERP (Lasso, BuildTopia, apparently now under ECI) and Zonda's site maps, renderings and the NewHomeSource marketplace ([Zonda](https://zondahome.com/)).

## Platform fees plus metered extras define pricing everywhere

Prices follow the same pattern in every segment: a platform or seat fee, then metered add-ons that earn the real margin. Only a minority of vendors publish prices. Lofty, BoldTrail, CINC (base), Ylopo, Rechat, Lone Wolf, AppFolio (per-unit), Rent Manager, Entrata, Sell.Do, Farvision, PropSpace and MRI Vault are all quote-only. That opacity drives the "mis-sold" complaints covered in section 4.

Three things follow from this. First, **calling and messaging are the wedge between tiers**. FUB's effective per-user price falls from $69 to about $33 at the Platform tier mainly because unlimited calling is bundled in, and AI ISAs are priced at roughly $49–200 a month. Second, **payments are the real business model in property management**. Buildium's incoming EFT fee drops from $2.35 to $0.60 as customers move up tiers, its e-sign fee from $5 per document to unlimited, and it charges $17–35 for screening ([Buildium](https://www.buildium.com/pricing/)). Fees are also an upgrade lever: DoorLoop makes incoming ACH free only on Premium ([DoorLoop](https://www.doorloop.com/pricing)). Third, **pricing by transaction or by file fits how small brokerages and TCs are paid**. At 40 files a month, Paperless Pipeline works out to about $6–7.60 per file, against $11–30 per file at ListedKit. A bundle should copy the fee model, since payments, screening, e-sign and insurance are what fund a cheap subscription. But it should publish every price and let customers cancel themselves, because opacity is a churn driver in its own right.

## Mobile breakage and contract traps drive churn more than missing features

The strongest churn evidence comes from App Store reviews written since 2024. Written reviews skew negative and mobile-specific, but the gap between all-time star ratings and recent reviews is large. That gap points to in-app rating prompts inflating store scores, and reviewers say so themselves: "4.8 is not real" ([App Store SkySlope](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1608944544)).

| Lofty | 3.93 (225) | 1.45 | 20/22 |

Source: [Apple iTunes Search API](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=kvcore&entity=software) and App Store review RSS, pulled 2026-10-08. BoomTown (4.92 across 12 reviews) and Real Geeks (5.0 across 3) had too few recent reviews to read churn from.

The first churn driver is **mobile apps that can't do what the desktop does**. Users then abandon the system of record. One BoldTrail user writes "I am just having to use my phone instead and bypass the CRM altogether," and another says they are "considering leaving my brokerage for one that uses a better CRM" ([App Store BoldTrail](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6502782766)). The second quote matters: a bad CRM can cost a brokerage its agents. FUB is "incredible" in the browser but takes "45-60 seconds… to load my inbox" on mobile, has no tasks, call log or dialer there, and lost lead ponds holding 30,000 leads on iPhone ([App Store FUB](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id966131499)). dotloop can't fill blanks or reliably handle multiple signers on mobile ([App Store dotloop](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id605565510)). Buildium reviewers "cannot access 95% of functions via app" ([App Store Buildium](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1288105237)). Yardi's maintenance app forces one technician to carry "2 phones at work" ([App Store Yardi](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id493578661)).

The second driver is **contracts, upsells and lock-in**. A kvCORE user changed brokerages and the vendor "refused to cancel my subscription service which was 1500 per month" ([App Store kvCORE](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1304578287)). Lofty users describe a 6-month lock-in, constant upsells and a dialer that charges "almost $150 if you want to send text messages" but won't dial unsaved numbers ([App Store Lofty](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1066013148)). BoldTrail was "misrepresented… as single-agent friendly." Zillow's control of the full workflow adds a neutrality concern: FUB's mobile app reportedly requires a Zillow Pro login ([App Store FUB](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id966131499)), and Zillow also owns dotloop and ShowingTime ([Wikipedia Zillow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zillow)).

The third driver is **the all-in-one paradox**. Lofty is "trying to be a one-stop shop… fails to deliver," and some users report "moving back to Follow Up Boss" ([App Store Lofty](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1066013148)). This is the strongest counter-argument to a bundle: breadth bought at the cost of depth gets punished. That is why the "no module weaker than the leader" rule is essential rather than aspirational.

The fourth driver applies to property management: **the tenant app shapes how renters see the landlord**. DoorLoop tenants complain about fees that are "different each time," the removal of split rent, and "10 minutes arguing with a stupid AI chat bot… rather than just filling out a simple form." One concludes that a landlord using the app is "cheap" ([App Store DoorLoop](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1590868801)). AppFolio logs managers out "in the middle of working on EMERGENCIES" ([App Store AppFolio](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1057763031)).

Structural shifts add new workflow demands. The NAR settlement ($418M; buyer agreements before touring from Aug 17, 2024) turns compensation into a field negotiated on every deal ([Wikipedia Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnett_v._National_Association_of_Realtors)). Compass's $1.6B acquisition of Anywhere, closed Jan 9, 2026, puts Coldwell Banker, Century 21, Sotheby's, Corcoran and BHGRE under one owner with its own technology. Anywhere previously had **300,000+ agents** ([Wikipedia Anywhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anywhere_Real_Estate)). That migration could push agents toward neutral, portable tools, but no reporting confirms this. AI use is real but shallow. Per the NAR 2026 Technology Report, **23% of Realtors use AI daily**, and AI users mainly apply it to listing descriptions (75%), social posts (56%) and emails (52%) ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/realtors-technology-report)). Reviews ask for basic reliability before more advanced AI.

## Seven innovation bets ranked by evidence strength

| Rank | Opportunity | Why it wins | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mobile-first apps with full parity**, including offline capture and persistent sessions, for agents, TCs, PMs and field techs | The single most consistent complaint across FUB, BoldTrail, dotloop, SkySlope, Buildium, AppFolio and Yardi | Strong (App Store, 7+ vendors) |
| 2 | **Unified inbox with auto-logging from the agent's own phone** (calls, SMS, email, WhatsApp, portal messages), plus dialing any number | Stops "bypass the CRM" data loss; fixes the Lofty and Sierra gaps; WhatsApp is already standard in India and UAE tools | Strong for US gaps; WhatsApp demand inferred from vendor features |
| 3 | **One record per household, property and deal across CRM, transaction, back office, PM and post-close** | Removes double data entry (SkySlope's two folders, FUB's broken household search) and unlocks lifecycle features: a past client becomes a seller lead, a landlord client, then a buyer of a new unit | Moderate (complaints plus architectural inference) |

| 5 | **Trust-first commercial model**: published prices, month-to-month, self-serve cancel, data that moves with the agent, transparent ad-spend and tenant-fee ledgers | Directly counters the BoldTrail, kvCORE, Lofty and DoorLoop complaints; quote-only pricing is the norm, so transparency stands out | Strong (reviews) |

The ranking puts boring reliability above AI on purpose. NAR data shows AI use concentrated on copywriting, and the angriest reviews are about broken notifications, lost notes and logouts. An AI ISA bolted onto an unreliable mobile app repeats BoldTrail's failure: the review that asks why AI hasn't made the app "unbeatable" is a 1-star review ([App Store BoldTrail](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6502782766)).

## One graph, sixteen modules, six personas

The architecture should be a single shared data graph with modules plugged in on top, not a suite of separate products linked by integrations. The core objects are **Person** (grouped into a **Household**), **Organization** (brokerage, team, PM firm, developer, CP firm, vendor), **Property** (a physical asset, split into **Units**), **Listing** (a sale or rental offering of a property or unit, with distribution rules), **Project** (a developer phase containing units, price lists and payment plans), **Deal** (a buyer, listing or new-home booking, holding the agreement, compensation, timeline and documents in one file), **Lease** (unit, residents, owner, ledger), **Ledger** (operating, trust, commission and collections accounts) and **Conversation** (every message and call, attached to whichever objects it concerns). Each module reads and writes the same objects. For example, a closed Deal automatically creates a Homebot-style homeowner record on the Property. A buyer who later rents out the home becomes an Owner on a Lease. A Project unit's booking creates a Deal and a collections Ledger. Permissions sit on Organization and role, so an agent's contacts and history are portable by default. Brokerage-owned data is flagged as brokerage-owned, which settles the "my CRM belongs to my old brokerage" problem at the schema level.

|---|---|---|---|
| Solo agent | 0, 3, 4, 5 (copilot), 8 (lite e-sign) | 1 IDX site, 2 lead gen, 6 seller tools, 11 nurture | FUB Grow; Wise Agent |

Packaging should copy what works and drop what drives churn. Price the core per seat, with lower effective rates at team scale as FUB does. Price PM per unit with a low floor (Breeze charges $1/unit with a $100 minimum). Offer per-file pricing for transaction work, as ListedKit and Paperless Pipeline do. Meter communications and AI usage openly, as Sell.Do does. Take fees on payments, screening and e-sign, but show them to tenants before they pay. Every module should be self-serve, month-to-month and cancellable without a phone call.

So is a possible 2025 Lone Wolf–MoxiWorks combination; the only verified fact is that MoxiWorks' founder joined Lone Wolf in July 2025. Homebot's ownership and prices are unconfirmed. The detailed NAR settlement terms (compensation stated as a specific, objective amount) and how vendors implemented them are unconfirmed; only the headline rules above are sourced. Nothing was found on the final outcome of Compass v. Zillow or on 2025 Clear Cooperation changes.

For property management, the following were not retrieved: the RealPage/DOJ settlement terms, HUD screening guidance, state trust-accounting rules, the Tenant Fees Act deposit cap, later Renters' Rights Act phases, and module lists for Hostaway, Fixflo, Alto and Reapit. For developers and other regions, nothing was sourced on: NoBroker, Housing.com, 99acres and MagicBricks builder packages; Salesforce builder implementations; Property Finder, Bayut and dubizzle pro tools; Trakheesi/Madmoun permits; off-plan payment-plan and escrow modules; PropertyGuru, Agentbox, REA and Domain agent products; Lasso and BuildTopia features; and design-centre selection tools. Evidence on pain points comes almost entirely from US App Store reviews, because G2, Capterra, TrustRadius and Reddit were blocked. Desktop complaints (IDX feed speed, reporting, integrations), how often each problem occurs across the user base, churn rates, NAR agent-count trends and tech spend per agent are all unmeasured. Several vendor claims in this report come from the vendors themselves and were not independently audited: Ylopo's 48% reply rate, Homebot's ROI, IRIS's 400 units in 6 hours, dotloop's 50% transaction share and MoxiWorks' 94% retention.

## Conclusion

Coverage is not the gap in this market. Between them, incumbents already sell nearly every feature a bundle needs, and the checklist above shows the bar is high in every module. The gap is coherence. Each segment's leader is a silo with a weak mobile app and a sales motion users resent, and the one vendor that tried full breadth natively (BoldTrail) is now the reviewers' cautionary tale. A bundle that wins will be judged less on how many modules it has than on whether a phone-only agent, a field technician and a launch-day CP can each finish their whole job on the same record without opening a laptop.

The most underrated opening is lifecycle continuity across segments, which no incumbent can offer because each owns only one stage. The same person moves from lead to buyer, to homeowner, to landlord, to new-project investor. Today that journey crosses four vendors and four databases. A shared graph turns every closed deal into a future listing, lease or booking, and that compounding value, more than any single AI feature, is what would make a modular bundle hard to leave for the right reasons.
